What Our Tree Trimming & Pruning Service Includes

Gulf Coast trees deal with salt wind, heavy canopies, and hurricane season. Proper pruning lowers the risk and keeps trees healthy, using correct cuts instead of shortcuts.

Live Oak Canopy Clearance

Raising and clearing the canopy over roofs, driveways, and sidewalks, with selective reduction of heavy, end-weighted limbs.

Deadwood Removal

Dead, broken, and hanging branches taken out of oaks and pines before they drop on roofs, vehicles, or play areas.

Wind Thinning

Selective thinning cuts that let wind pass through the canopy, so a dense tree catches less wind ahead of storm season.

No Topping or Lion-Tailing

We make clean cuts at the proper branch junction, never stubbing limbs or stripping the interior, which weakens trees and invites decay.

Signs Your Tree Needs Trimming

  • Limbs touching or scraping the roof: Branches rubbing shingles, gutters, or siding can damage the roof and hold moisture against it.
  • Deadwood & hanging limbs: Bare, brittle, or broken branches high in the canopy over roofs, driveways, or play areas.
  • A dense, heavy canopy before storm season: Thick crowns catch wind like a sail, and selective thinning helps reduce that load.
  • Low or crossing branches: Limbs blocking driveways, sidewalks, or sightlines, or rubbing against each other and wearing through the bark.

Tree Trimming & Pruning FAQs

Do you offer free tree trimming estimates?

Yes. We come out, look at the tree and surrounding structures, answer your questions, and give you a clear price at no cost.

How do you clear a live oak canopy off my roof?

We raise the canopy and prune back to proper branch junctions, taking limbs off in sections and lowering them on ropes. We remove only what the tree can handle, so it stays healthy and keeps its shape.

Can you remove deadwood from a tall oak or pine?

Yes. We climb the tree and remove dead, broken, and hanging branches, lowering each piece on ropes so nothing lands on your roof, vehicles, or landscaping.

What is wind thinning, and does it help before hurricane season?

Wind thinning is the selective removal of small interior branches so wind passes through the canopy instead of pushing against it. It reduces wind load but won't make a tree storm-proof, and a tree that is structurally unsound may need removal instead. We'll tell you honestly which applies.

Do you top trees or lion-tail them?

No. Topping (cutting main limbs back to stubs) and lion-tailing (stripping the interior branches and leaving tufts at the ends) stress the tree and lead to weak regrowth and decay. We use proper cuts that preserve the tree's structure.

What if limbs are touching or tangled in power lines?

Never approach limbs touching or tangled in a power line. If primary utility lines are involved, contact your electric utility first so they can make the line safe before tree work begins.
